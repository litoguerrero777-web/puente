Cómo funciona · How it works

The PUENTE Playbook

A pilot cooperation between ACAMC (Facultad de Medicina, Universidad de Cartagena) and SACNAS (Baylor University) to build English proficiency for the ECFMG pathway — the OET Medicine exam and the USMLE Steps — through weekly one-to-one practice, November 16 to December 10, 2026 — wrapping up right before Baylor finals.

Getting started

  1. Choose your track. Cartagena students pick Clinical English (OSCE-style patient encounters) or Conversational English (cultural exchange and everyday fluency). You keep the same track for the whole pilot.
  2. Register on the Partner Matching page. Add your name and email under your school and track. Partners are set for the full pilot — no weekly rotation — so you build rapport and can track real progress.
  3. Propose a meeting time each week. Both partners pick a date and a time slot from the template menus (no free-typing, so there are never punctuation or format mix-ups). When both selections are identical the partnership row turns Confirmed; if they differ it turns Different times until you adjust.
  4. Meet once a week by video (45–60 minutes). Your pair's standing room link lives on the Meet Links page — one click to join, no account or host needed. Aim to confirm your slot by Friday of the week before.
  5. Rate and reflect. After week 1 and week 4 (the final week), each partner completes the Proficiency Survey about the other — a baseline and a final picture of growth.

Clinical track · session protocol

Materials for each week are on the Weekly Materials page. Every case is AI-generated and reviewed for the OET Medicine / USMLE clinical-communication style.

  1. Before the call: the Baylor (SACNAS) partner downloads the week's Full Guideline (narrative, explanations, answers, benchmark highlights). The Cartagena (ACAMC) partner downloads only the Benchmark Sheet — the checklist of things to accomplish in the encounter.
  2. Split screen: on the call, the Baylor partner keeps the guideline open beside the video. The Cartagena partner keeps only the benchmarks visible, and opens the full narrative only if truly stuck. The surprise is the point — it mirrors a real OSCE station.
  3. Role-play (≈25 min): the Baylor partner plays the patient using the narrative script; the Cartagena partner leads the encounter in English, working through the benchmarks.
  4. Debrief (≈15 min): go benchmark by benchmark. The Baylor partner shares the model phrases and answers from the guideline; both flag pronunciation, vocabulary, and phrasing to practice.
  5. Wrap (≈5 min): agree next week's slot and enter it on the Partner Matching page before you hang up.

Conversational track · session protocol

Download the week's prompt set. Take turns asking; follow tangents — the prompts are a springboard, not a script. Each set names a grammar focus (past narration, conditionals, present perfect…): the native/fluent speaker gently recasts errors on that pattern rather than correcting every slip. Swap languages for the last ten minutes if the Baylor partner is practicing Spanish — a bridge carries traffic both ways.

Ground rules

Be on time; message your partner ahead if you must move a session. Cameras on when possible. Errors are material, not embarrassments. Everything shared stays between partners. If a partnership isn't working, write to the coordinators — no hard feelings, quick rematch.

Evaluación · Proficiency survey

Proficiency Survey

After week 1 (baseline) and week 4 (final), rate your partner's English performance using the scale below. Submissions are saved on this page for the coordinators, and you can also send a copy by email to the address you choose.

The scale

ScoreWhat it looks like in the session
5Communicates effortlessly; precise vocabulary; errors are rare and self-corrected; fully natural pace. (≈ CEFR C1–C2)
4Communicates clearly with occasional errors that never block understanding; good range; rarely searches for words. (≈ B2+)
3Gets the message across with effort; noticeable errors and pauses; sometimes needs rephrasing or repetition. (≈ B1–B2)
2Basic exchanges succeed; frequent errors and long pauses; relies on the partner to carry the conversation. (≈ A2–B1)
1Isolated words and memorized phrases; communication frequently breaks down. (≈ A1–A2)
